## GATHER — the deep dive

The batch doc called GATHER "the highest risk in the set … exactly the shape
that gets registered in Class 16." That was right, though not for the reason
anticipated. Nothing *contains* or *is contained in* GATHER — it is one word,
so containment collapses to exact match. It fails on accumulation instead, and
the accumulation is unusually thick:

**On the register.** 92 records carry the exact literal element `GATHER`.
Eleven are live in Cl 16 or Cl 9. The two that matter were both filed by
**IF Gathering on 2026-01-08**:

- **`sn99584547`, Class 016** — "Printed publications, namely, books, booklets,
  and teaching materials in the field of faith, religion and spirituality."
  **Published for Opposition on 2026-07-28** — eight days before this screen.
  Absent an opposition it registers.
- **`sn99584504`, Class 009** — "Downloadable electronic publications in the
  nature of **conversation cards**, event guides, articles, newsletters, and
  books in the field of faith, religion and spirituality." Under examination.

The Class 9 recitation names *conversation cards* — which is P1's product, in
P1's class. That is closer to this batch than anything Batch 3 turned up.

**What cuts the other way.** Both applications are restricted to "the field of
faith, religion and spirituality." A registration so limited does not, on a
formal reading, block secular Thanksgiving place cards. The remaining nine live
Cl 9 marks are software in plainly unrelated fields — drone inventory analytics,
video-conferencing hardware, vehicle location sharing, survey tooling, meal
planning. And the only Cl 016 `GATHER` registration ever granted (Ugmonk,
desktop organizers, Reg 5352145) was **cancelled 2024-06-21**, so Class 16 has
no incumbent registrant today.

**What tips it.** Etsy. The shop sweep returned 943 matches for `gather`, and
the actively-trading ones cluster precisely in our product category:

| GatheredLovePaperie | 855 |
| gatheredprintshop | 421 |

Eight Gather-branded shops selling prints and paper goods, all trading. Measured
against the Dog Mama precedent that set this rule, this is far *stronger* than
the four dormant-handle collisions Batch 3 passed over — those had zero active
listings between them and were reserved names, not traders. These are traders,
in our category, using our word as their brand.

**Verdict.** FAIL. A pending Cl 016 publication, a Cl 009 filing naming
conversation cards, and eight actively-trading Gather-branded print shops is too
much to draw art against — and the batch doc already supplies the escape:
*"Fallback if screening kills the standalone words: leaf motifs + name line
only, zero text. The product survives without the words."*

P2 also survives on `THANKFUL` and `GRATEFUL`, both of which passed cleanly. The
recommendation is to ship the two words that cleared plus the zero-text variant,
and drop the GATHER variant rather than replace it — three place-card designs is
the same product either way.

This is a judgment call, recorded here so it can be overridden with eyes open.
The formal trademark reading — ornamental use of a dictionary word on a secular
seasonal product, against field-restricted religious-publishing applications —
is defensible. What is not defensible is doing it *unknowingly*.

## The Etsy shop-name finding

Three phrases have an **exact** shop-name match: `gather` (0 active),
`PumpkinPie` (0 active), `GratitudeJar` (0 active). Per the Batch-3 rule, all
three are reserved handles rather than traders and generate no common-law
rights, so none of them moves a verdict on its own.

The signal that mattered was not exact names but **containing names that trade**:

- **GATHER** — eight print/paper shops with 74–855 active listings each. This
  contributed to the FAIL above.
- **PUMPKIN PIE** — `PumpkinPiePrintsShop` (90 active) and `PumpkinPiePrintables`
  (18 active) sell printables under a Pumpkin-Pie-branded name. Noted, but not
  disqualifying: "pumpkin pie" is the name of a dessert, our use is a recipe-card
  title identifying the recipe, and the register agrees it is descriptive —
  the Cl 016 application for it was refused.
- **THANKFUL / GRATEFUL** — 35 and 78 containing-and-active shops respectively,
  but **none** with a paper-goods, print or stationery name. Both cleared.
- **TURKEY DAY** — zero shops, either spelling.

## Method, and what it cannot tell you

Two channels, because neither alone is sound — unchanged from Batch 3:

1. **Discovery — Justia's full-text mirror of the USPTO register**, fetched with
   a full browser header set. Each phrase was searched plus its containing
   sub-phrases; GATHER additionally got a deep pass (15 result pages plus four
   goods-targeted queries such as `gather stationery` and `gather place cards`)
   because a single 100-result sweep of a 500-result index is not coverage.
2. **Verification — USPTO TSDR `statusview`, per serial.** Justia's cards carry
   no live/dead status at all, so nothing above is decided on them. **228 unique
   serials were fetched and parsed.**

Three method notes from this session:

- **The parser was validated against known answers before it was trusted.**
  Three Batch-3 controls were re-run: `BURN AFTER READING` (sn99073088, expect
  LIVE Cl 004), `GRATITUDE GLASS JAR` (sn87649764, expect DEAD), and the
  snippet-lies control `LOVE LANGUAGE` (sn90185577, expect DEAD despite reading
  live in every search result). All three came back correct.
- **Justia now blocks Node's `fetch` outright while serving `curl` the identical
  request.** The block keys on the TLS/HTTP2 fingerprint, not the headers. Read
  naively this is the 403-as-absence bug again in a new place: a blocked
  discovery pass returns zero results, which looks exactly like a clean phrase.
  Discovery shells out to `curl` and treats any non-200 as `RATE-LIMITED`.
- **TSDR held up under the full 228-serial run** with 1.5s spacing and
  exponential backoff — **zero 403s**. Every serial cited above was actually
  read. Had any been refused it would appear as `RATE-LIMITED` here, not as a
  pass.

**Limits.** Discovery depends on a third-party full-text index capped at 500
results per query, so a mark it does not surface is a mark this screen did not
see — most relevant for `GATHER` and `GRATEFUL`, where the index reported 500+
matches. Searches were English literal-element only, covering neither design
marks nor foreign equivalents. Classes 16 and 9 were screened as instructed; a
live mark in another class does not appear as a blocker here. The Etsy sweep
returns at most 100 shops per query, so containing-name counts are floors, not
totals — and it reads shop *names*, not what those shops actually sell. This is
a screen, not legal advice.

## Standing caveats carried forward

- **GRATEFUL is cleared for paper goods only.** `GRATEFUL` is live and
  registered in **Class 25** (Reg 3956608, GRATEFUL APPAREL INC.). If a later
  wave moves this word onto a tee or tote it must be re-screened and will very
  likely fail. Same hazard as Batch 3's `EMOTIONALLY UNAVAILABLE` note — the
  habit of reusing artwork across product types makes this live, not theoretical.
- **THANKFUL's Class 16 slot is newly vacant.** Reg 4770832 covered stationery,
  greeting cards and blank note cards and was cancelled only on **2026-03-06**.
  Nothing is live there today, which is why it passes — but a vacancy that fresh
  can be refiled by anyone, including the former registrant. Worth re-checking
  before any *second* Thanksgiving season, not before this one.
- **GATHER should not be quietly revived** in a later batch on the reasoning
  that "no mark actually blocks it." Re-read the deep dive first; if
  `sn99584547` has registered by then, the Class 16 picture is worse than it is
  today, not better.
