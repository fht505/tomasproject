## COLD OPEN

[broll: dipstick pulled and wiped, level below the hash marks; clean garage floor underneath]

Last episode, the driveway told the story. This time the driveway is
spotless — and the dipstick keeps coming up low anyway. A quart gone
every thousand miles, fifteen hundred, and no stain anywhere.

That oil didn't leak. It went *through the engine* and out the tailpipe
as smoke you mostly can't see. Burning oil is the leak with no puddle —
and it runs on a completely different clock than the gasket stories.
Some of it is normal. Some of it is a countdown. The difference is
rate, color, and timing — all checkable from the driver's seat.

## THE SYMPTOM, PRECISELY

[callout: three-way split — "How fast? (quarts per 1,000 mi)" / "Blue smoke — when exactly?" / "Startup puff vs under load"]

**Rate first — and write it down.** Full mark, note the mileage, check
every fuel stop. Manufacturers publish oil-consumption specs that
surprise people — a quart in a thousand-plus miles is inside "normal"
for plenty of modern engines, especially turbos. [TECH CONFIRM — the
consumption threshold where YOU start caring: quarts per how many
miles?] Below the threshold: monitor. Above: this episode.

**Color: burning oil smokes BLUE-GRAY.** Not white (coolant steam,
ep 4), not black (rich fuel). Blue is oil's signature — subtle, easiest
seen in mirrors under hard acceleration or by the car behind you.

**Timing is the diagnosis.** A puff at STARTUP that clears says one
thing; smoke under ACCELERATION says another; smoke on DECELERATION —
lifting off the gas downhill — says a third. Remember which one you
have; the whole causes section is organized by it.

## THE MECHANISM — HOW OIL GETS INTO THE FIRE

[cutaway: cylinder closeup — piston with ring pack sealing against cylinder wall; above, valves sliding in guides with seals; oil mist everywhere outside the chamber]

The combustion chamber is supposed to be an oil-free zone, guarded at
two borders. Below: the **piston rings** — springy bands around each
piston that scrape the cylinder walls clean on every stroke. Above: the
**valve stem seals** — little rubber caps where the valves slide down
into the chamber. Oil mist fills everything *outside* those borders,
lubricating constantly. Burning oil means a border is letting it
through — and WHICH border is what the smoke-timing tells you.

## CAUSE #1 — WORN VALVE STEM SEALS (the startup puff)

[flow: engine parked overnight — oil seeping down valve stems past hardened seals, pooling on valve heads; morning start burns it: blue puff, then clear]

Park overnight and gravity goes to work: oil seeps past hardened valve
seals, pooling on top of the closed valves. First start of the morning
burns that pool off — a **blue puff at startup that clears in seconds**.
That's the valve-seal signature: dramatic-looking, slow-moving, and the
most benign entry in this episode. Consumption stays modest; many cars
puff for years. [TECH CONFIRM — agree startup-puff cars can often run
long-term, or do you counsel repair sooner?]

## CAUSE #2 — WORN OR STUCK PISTON RINGS (the real consumption)

[cutaway: ring pack — worn rings with poor wall contact; oil passing upward on intake stroke; blue smoke under load]

The rings are the big border, and when they wear — or gum up and stick
in their grooves, a famous issue on some direct-injection engines
[TECH CONFIRM — want to name engine families FHT sees for oil
consumption, or keep it brand-neutral?] — oil rides up the cylinder
walls into the burn. Signature: **blue smoke under acceleration** and
consumption that climbs with every oil change interval. Confirmation is
a **compression test or leak-down test** — the cylinders tell on
themselves. This is the structural version of burning oil: it doesn't
heal, it trends. [TECH CONFIRM — leak-down vs compression: your
preferred confirm for ring wear?]

## CAUSE #3 — PCV SYSTEM FAILURE (the impostor with a cheap fix)

[flow: PCV path — crankcase vapors routed through valve back to intake; stuck valve pulling raw oil mist into the intake tract]

Before condemning rings, check the impostor. The **PCV system** recycles
crankcase vapors back into the intake — through a valve that's supposed
to meter the flow. When it sticks open, the intake vacuums raw oil mist
straight into the chambers: sudden oil consumption, blue smoke, oily
residue in the intake tract — on an engine whose rings are fine. The
part is often absurdly cheap. Any sudden-onset oil burning: PCV first,
always, before anyone says the word "rebuild." [TECH CONFIRM — a PCV
save story would land great here; also typical PCV part/labor at FHT]

## CAUSE #4 — TURBO SHAFT SEALS (for the boosted crowd)

[cutaway: turbocharger — shaft between exhaust and intake wheels, oil-fed bearing in the middle, seals leaking into both housings]

Turbos spin on a shaft lubricated by engine oil at both ends, sealed
from the air path. Worn turbo seals leak oil INTO the intake or exhaust
stream: blue smoke (often on deceleration or right after), oil pooling
in the intercooler piping, and a whistle that's changed character.
On turbo cars this sits high on the suspect list — and it's a turbo
job, not an engine job, which matters for the bill. [TECH CONFIRM —
how much turbo work does FHT see? Keep/trim this section accordingly]

## CAUSE #5 — JUST DRIVING IT LOW (the self-inflicted accelerant)

[callout: feedback loop — low oil → hotter, dirtier remaining oil → faster wear → burns more → lower]

The quiet one: an engine run chronically a quart-plus low concentrates
heat and contaminants in the oil that remains, accelerating exactly the
ring and seal wear that burns more oil. Consumption problems *compound*
if you don't keep the level up while deciding what to do. Checking and
topping up isn't ignoring the problem — it's buying time at four
dollars a quart. [TECH CONFIRM — bless this framing]

## THE REPAIR — WHAT THE MONEY LOOKS LIKE

[flow: decision tree — smoke timing in, jobs out]

Cheapest to dearest: **PCV valve** (parts money). **Valve stem seals**
(mid; on many engines doable without pulling the head). **Turbo
replacement/rebuild** (mid-to-large, but contained). **Piston rings**
(engine-open territory — on high-mileage cars this triggers the same
repair-vs-replace conversation as the cracked head in ep 4).
And the modern middle path worth naming: some consumption cases respond
to **piston-cleaning treatments/oil system flushes** targeted at stuck
rings [TECH CONFIRM — does FHT use/endorse any de-coking or flush

## URGENT OR IGNORABLE?

[callout: three-tier verdict card — "Monitor + top up" / "Diagnose this month" / "Engine off"]

**Monitor + top up:** consumption below the caring threshold, startup
puff only, level held between checks. Keep a quart in the trunk and a
log on your phone.

**Diagnose this month:** consumption trending worse across intervals,
smoke under load, sudden-onset burning (PCV check!), or a turbo tell.
Trends don't reverse themselves.

**Engine off:** oil light, knocking, or consumption so fast you're
adding quarts weekly. That's not consumption anymore; that's an engine
actively failing.

## OUTRO

So: no puddle doesn't mean no problem — it means the oil is exiting
through the fire. The rate says whether to care, the blue says it's
oil, and the timing — startup, load, or lift-off — points at seals,
rings, PCV, or turbo. Check the cheap impostor first, keep the level
up while you decide, and let a compression test referee the big calls.

That's both oil stories — the leak and the burn. Next time, the
