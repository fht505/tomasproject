## COLD OPEN

[broll: quiet suburban street, morning, car backing out of a driveway]

Six-thirty in the morning. You back out of the driveway, touch the brakes,
and your car announces you to the entire neighborhood — a squeal you can
feel in your teeth.

Here's the strange part: sometimes that squeak means absolutely nothing.
And sometimes it's your brakes *deliberately* making that noise — on
purpose, by design — to tell you something. By the end of this video
you'll know how to tell the difference.

[callout: channel card — "Why Is My Car Doing That?"]

## THE SYMPTOM, PRECISELY

Squeaks come in flavors, and the flavor is the diagnosis. Ask yourself
three questions.

[callout: three-way split — "When does it squeak?" / "Cold or warm?" / "Does it stop when you brake harder?"]

**One: when?** A squeal *only while braking* is a brake noise. A squeal
that runs constantly while driving and *disappears* when you press the
pedal — that's a very specific message, and we'll get to it, because it's
the one your brakes make on purpose.

**Two: cold or warm?** A squeak on the first few stops of a cold, damp
morning that vanishes once everything warms up is usually the most
innocent noise a car can make.

**Three: pressure.** Light-pedal squeal that goes quiet when you press
harder points one direction; a grind or growl that gets *worse* with
pressure points somewhere much more expensive.

## THE MECHANISM — WHY BRAKES SING AT ALL

[cutaway: front corner assembly — the ep-1 3D scene, camera pushing to the pad/rotor contact]

Quick physics, because it makes everything else obvious. A brake squeal
is a vibration — the pad, caliper, and rotor form a system that can
resonate like a violin string when the pad slides across the disc. At a
few thousand vibrations per second, steel-on-friction-material sings in
exactly the frequency band human ears hate most. [TECH CONFIRM — anything
you'd add about which components you find actually resonating most often?]

The engineering question is never "why is it vibrating" — everything
vibrates. It's "what changed, so that the vibration stopped being damped?"

## CAUSE #1 — SURFACE RUST AFTER RAIN OR A CAR WASH (most common, most innocent)

[flow: rotor surface strip from ep 1, thin orange film building overnight, first stops scrubbing it clean]

Steel rotors rust *overnight*. A humid night, rain, a car wash before
parking — by morning there's an invisible orange film on the discs. The
first few stops scrub it off, and that scrubbing is your morning squeal.
Gone in half a mile. This is the noise that means nothing. If your squeak
only shows up cold and damp and always fades — you can stop watching this
video. [TECH CONFIRM — agree that's dischargeable advice, or do you want a
caveat here?]

## CAUSE #2 — THE WEAR INDICATOR (the squeal that's on purpose)

[cutaway: pad side-view — friction material wearing down toward a small steel tab, tab touching rotor]

Inside most brake pads is a little steel tab called a **wear indicator**,
set at the legal-minimum pad thickness. When the friction material wears
down to it, the tab drags on the rotor and produces a constant, high
squeal while you drive — that *stops* when you press the pedal, because
braking pressure changes the contact.

That pattern — sings while cruising, silent while braking — is your car
saying: **pads are done, book the appointment.** Not an emergency today.
An emergency if you ignore it for a month, because after the squealer
comes the grinding — and grinding means the pad backing plate is eating
your rotor. [TECH CONFIRM — typical time/miles between "squealer starts"
and "metal on metal" in your experience?]

## CAUSE #3 — GLAZED PADS OR CHEAP PADS

[cutaway: pad magnified — surface shiny and crystallized vs healthy matte surface]

Pads that got cooked — a mountain descent, a panic stop, riding the
brakes — can **glaze**: the friction surface crystallizes into a hard,
shiny layer that squeals like chalk on a board and, worse, *grips less*.
Budget pads with high metal content do the same thing from day one.
Glazing often shows up WITH a change in pedal feel — takes more push to
stop the same way. [TECH CONFIRM — your shop's take on economy vs
mid-tier pads; do you see glazing more on a specific brand/type?]

## CAUSE #4 — MISSING HARDWARE: SHIMS, CLIPS, AND LUBE

[cutaway: exploded pad assembly — shim, abutment clips, slide points highlighted]

Between the pad and caliper piston there's supposed to be a **shim** — a
thin damper whose whole job is killing the vibration we talked about. Pads
sit in **abutment clips**, and their contact points get a dab of brake
grease at install. A rushed brake job that skips the shims, reuses old
rusty clips, or skips the lube produces a brand-new brake that squeals
worse than the old one. If your squeak started *right after* a brake
job — this is the prime suspect, and it's a warranty-of-workmanship
conversation, not a new-parts conversation. [TECH CONFIRM — how often do
squeak complaints trace to missing/skipped hardware from a previous shop?]

## CAUSE #5 — STICKING CALIPER OR RUSTY SLIDE PINS

[cutaway: caliper cross-section from ep 1 — seized slide pin, pad dragging lightly]

The same sticking caliper from our brake-shake episode can whistle instead
of shake: a lightly-dragging pad hums or squeals at speed, brake or no
brake, often with one wheel running hot and fuel economy dipping. Squeal
plus pull to one side, or squeal plus a hot wheel — caliper service, this
week.

## THE REPAIR — WHAT FIXES WHAT

[flow: repair decision tree — noise pattern in, fix out]

Match the fix to the flavor:

- Morning-damp squeal: **no repair exists or is needed.**
- Wear-indicator squeal: **pads, and usually rotors, that axle.** Booked
  soon, priced as routine maintenance — the cheapest brake job you'll
  ever get is the one before the grinding starts.
- Glazed or cheap pads: **new quality pads**, often with a rotor
  resurface or replacement so the fresh pads bed into a clean surface.
- Post-brake-job squeal: **back to the installer.** Shims, clips, lube.
- Sticking caliper: **caliper service or replacement** plus whatever the
  dragging cooked.

## URGENT OR IGNORABLE?

[callout: three-tier verdict card — "Ignore it" / "Book it" / "This week"]

**Ignore it:** cold-morning squeal that fades with the first few stops.
The one noise on this channel you're allowed to do nothing about.

**Book it:** the constant cruise-squeal that quiets under braking — the
wear indicator. You have some runway, and none to waste.

**This week:** squeal with a hot wheel, a pull, pedal-feel changes — or
any squeal that's become a **grind**. Grinding is metal on metal; every
mile is money.

## OUTRO

So: damp-morning squeals are rust scrubbing itself off, a cruise-squeal
that stops when you brake is your pads' built-in appointment reminder,
and a squeal that arrived with a hot wheel or a pull is a caliper waving
for help. Your brakes talk. Now you speak the language.

Next time: your brake pedal is pulsing under your foot — and the villain
is a rotor that isn't the same thickness everywhere. That one's stranger
than it sounds.
