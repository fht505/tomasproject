## COLD OPEN

[broll: dark stain on a driveway, morning light, car backing away to reveal it]

There's a stain on the driveway. It's been there a while, growing by a
coin-size a week, and you've developed a relationship with it: you park
a little differently so you don't have to look at it.

Here's what that stain actually is: information leaking out of the one
system in your car that everything else depends on. And here's the good
news — oil leaks are the most *diagnosable* leaks a car has, because oil
is honest. It drips straight down, it stains where it exits, and
gravity writes the report. Ten minutes, and you'll read stains like a
tech does.

[callout: channel card — "Why Is My Car Doing That?"]

## THE SYMPTOM, PRECISELY

[callout: three-way split — "Fresh honey or black coffee?" / "Where under the car?" / "Drips parked, or burns driving?"]

**Color first.** Fresh oil is honey-amber; used oil is dark brown to
black. Either way it's slick, doesn't smell sweet (that's coolant, last
episode), and rainbow-sheens on wet pavement. Red fluid is transmission
or power steering — different episodes. [TECH CONFIRM — want a quick
fluid-color chart beat here? It's a classic shop-wall poster moment]

**Location second.** Front-center under the engine, along the sides,
toward the back where engine meets transmission — each zone has its own
suspects. The ep-6 cardboard trick works even better for oil because it
can't flash off hot parts as steam.

**Third: does the level actually drop between changes?** A seep that
never moves the dipstick is a cosmetic problem. A quart every few weeks
is a real leak — or it's *burning*, which is next episode's story. The
dipstick is the referee. [TECH CONFIRM — the seep-vs-leak threshold you
use: what loss rate makes it worth fixing vs watching?]

## THE MECHANISM — WHY ENGINES LEAK AT THE SEAMS

[cutaway: engine from ep 4, new emphasis — oil sump at bottom, oil pathways, and every gasket plane glowing: valve cover, oil pan, front/rear seals, filter/drain interface]

An engine is metal castings bolted together with **gaskets** — soft
sealing layers — at every joint, plus rotating shafts exiting the block
through rubber **seals**. Oil is pumped everywhere inside at pressure
while heat cycles bake every gasket ten thousand times. Rubber and cork
harden, shrink, and crack with age; every leak in this episode is some
version of that sentence. Which is why oil leaks are overwhelmingly an
*age and mileage* story, not a defect story.

## LEAK SITE #1 — VALVE COVER GASKET (the champion)

[cutaway: top of engine — valve cover perimeter glowing, seep trails running down the block sides, drips onto exhaust manifold with smoke wisp]

The valve cover sits on top of the engine like a lid, and its perimeter
gasket is the most common oil leak in the business. Tells: oil wetness
around the top edges of the engine, streaks running DOWN the block
sides, and — the signature — a **burning-oil smell after driving**,
because the seep drips onto the hot exhaust manifold and smokes. Light
blue wisps from under the hood after parking, without overheating: this
is usually it. [TECH CONFIRM — agree valve cover is your #1 walk-in oil
leak? And the burning-smell tell?]

## LEAK SITE #2 — OIL PAN GASKET AND DRAIN PLUG

[cutaway: underside — pan perimeter and drain plug glowing, drip forming at the pan's lowest corner]

The pan holds the oil at the bottom, sealed by its own perimeter gasket,
with the **drain plug** as a deliberate hole that gets opened every
change. Stripped plug threads, a crushed or reused washer, or a plug
torqued by feel at a quickie lube — the classic post-oil-change leak.
If your stain appeared within days of a change: plug and filter first,
always. [TECH CONFIRM — how many post-quick-lube leak fixes does FHT see
a month? Anonymized pattern makes a great trust beat]

## LEAK SITE #3 — THE OIL FILTER INTERFACE

[cutaway: filter mount — double-gasket failure mode shown: old ring stuck to block, new filter sealing against it, oil spraying under pressure]

The filter seals with a rubber ring — and it has a famous failure mode:
the old ring **sticks to the block**, the new filter goes on over it,
and the double-gasket seal holds just long enough to leave the shop.
Then it lets go at pressure, and the car dumps oil fast, with the oil
light coming on. This is the leak that goes from "none" to "emergency"
in one drive. Any sudden major leak right after an oil change: engine
off, check filter area first. [TECH CONFIRM — seen the double-gasket
special recently? Frequency?]

## LEAK SITE #4 — FRONT AND REAR MAIN SEALS (the aging engine's leaks)

[cutaway: crankshaft exiting block both ends — front seal behind pulley, rear main at the transmission joint, drip at the bellhousing seam]

The crankshaft exits the engine at both ends through rubber lip seals.
The **front seal** leaks behind the crank pulley; the **rear main**
leaks into the bellhousing between engine and transmission — appearing
as a drip from that seam, where it's easily confused with a
transmission leak. These are the high-mileage seep sites, and the rear
main is the famous one because the labor to reach it (transmission out,
on most cars) dwarfs the five-dollar part. Honesty beat: a *seeping*
(not dripping) rear main on a high-mileage car is often a
monitor-don't-fix call. [TECH CONFIRM — your rear-main counsel: at what
point do you tell customers to fix vs live with it?]

## LEAK SITE #5 — PRESSURE SENSORS AND COOLER LINES (the small-parts tier)

[cutaway: block-mounted oil pressure sender weeping; oil cooler lines and their crimp fittings]

A grab bag the flashlight finds: the **oil pressure sender** (a small
screw-in sensor that weeps from its body when its diaphragm ages),
**oil cooler lines** and their crimped fittings on cars that have them,
and on some engines, timing-cover seams. Small parts, honest fixes,
often mistaken for scarier leaks because oil travels and coats
everything below its exit. Rule: the leak is at the *highest, cleanest*
wet point — everything below is just plumbing by gravity.
[TECH CONFIRM — bless "highest cleanest wet point" as the hunting rule;
it's the episode's key transferable skill]

## THE REPAIR — WHAT THE MONEY LOOKS LIKE

[flow: decision tree — leak site in, job scale out]

The pattern by now: seals are cheap, *reaching* them is the bill. Valve
cover: modest. Pan gasket: modest-to-mid (some engines want subframe
work). Filter/plug issues: trivial. Front seal: mid, often bundled with
timing/accessory work while everything's apart. Rear main: the big one,
labor-dominated — which is why the fix-vs-monitor conversation exists.

## URGENT OR IGNORABLE?

[callout: three-tier verdict card — "Watch it" / "Book it" / "Engine off"]

**Watch it:** seepage — dampness without drips, dipstick stable between
changes, no smoke smell. Photograph it, recheck monthly.

**Book it:** an actual drip pattern, level moving between changes, or
the burning smell after parking. Leaks onto hot exhaust are also a
(small but real) fire consideration — worth saying plainly.
[TECH CONFIRM — comfortable with the fire mention at this tier?]

**Engine off:** the oil light, a sudden puddle, or a knock-tick under
the hood with a fresh leak. The oil light is not an advisory — below
pressure, engines eat themselves in minutes. Off, tow, no heroics.

## OUTRO

So: oil leaks are gravity's honest report. The color names the fluid,
the highest clean wet point names the exit, the dipstick referees
whether it matters, and the calendar (right after an oil change?) names
the suspect list. Most of them are gasket age, not engine doom — and
the one light that means stop means STOP.
