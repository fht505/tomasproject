## COLD OPEN

[broll: parking lot, slow full-lock turn, window down]

Roll through a parking lot with the wheel cranked, window down, and
your car performs its little concert: maybe a click-click-click, maybe
a groan, maybe a whine that follows the wheel. Straighten out — silence.

Turning is when your front end works hardest: joints articulate to
their limits, weight shifts onto one side, and parts that pass the
straight-line test get cross-examined. Which is great news for
diagnosis — because each part fails with its own *specific sound*. This
episode is an ear-training course: five noises, five names, five very
different bills.

## THE SYMPTOM, PRECISELY

[callout: sound-map card — "CLICKING in tight turns" / "HUM/GROWL in sweeping turns" / "WHINE with the wheel" / "CLUNK over bumps while turning" / "CREAK/GROAN low speed"]

Five sounds, and WHEN each speaks:

- **Click-click-click**, rhythmic with wheel speed, in TIGHT turns
  (parking lots, U-turns)
- **Hum or growl** in fast SWEEPING turns (highway ramps) that changes
  when you swing the wheel the other way
- **Whine** that rises and falls with how far you turn the wheel,
  especially at full lock
- **Clunk or pop** when turning AND hitting a bump together
- **Creak/groan** like an old ship, low speed, worst after sitting

Match your noise now; the causes below run in that order.

## CAUSE #1 — CLICKING: THE CV JOINT (the parking-lot drumbeat)

[cutaway: NEW 3D — CV axle: shaft, constant-velocity joint ball cage, rubber boot; boot torn, grease flung, grit inside]

Front-wheel-drive cars send power through **CV (constant velocity)
joints** — caged ball bearings that let the axle bend with steering
and suspension while spinning. Each lives inside a rubber **boot** full
of grease. The boot tears with age, grease flings out, grit gets in,
and the dry joint starts announcing tight turns: *click-click-click*
in time with wheel speed.

The inspection beat: a torn boot with flung grease is visible with a
flashlight behind the front wheels — and caught at TORN-BOOT stage
(before the clicking starts) it's a cheap reboot job instead of an
axle. Clicking already? The joint's eating itself; axle replacement
territory, and the noise only grows. [TECH CONFIRM — do you still
reboot caught-early joints, or is replacement-axle-always the modern

## CAUSE #2 — HUM/GROWL IN SWEEPING TURNS: THE WHEEL BEARING RETURNS

[cutaway: hub/bearing from ep 1 — load shifting onto outer wheel in a sweeper; growl waves loudest with weight on the bad side]

Episode 3's impostor gets its turn as the headliner. In a sweeping
turn, the car's weight rolls onto the outside wheel — and a worn
bearing under load hums, growls, or roars. The steering-input version
of the S-weave test: noise that gets LOUDER turning one direction and
QUIETER the other names the loaded side. [TECH CONFIRM — restate: the
noisy-when-loaded side is the bad bearing, correct? People get this
backwards constantly]

Bearing urgency recap from ep 3 applies: loud bearings are a
today-not-someday item — wheels depend on them. [TECH CONFIRM — has
FHT seen a genuinely failed/separated bearing case worth a 10-second
cautionary mention?]

## CAUSE #3 — WHINE WITH THE WHEEL: POWER STEERING

[flow: steering path — pump (hydraulic) pulling fluid, whining at low level/full lock; EPS note for electric cars]

A whine that tracks your steering input — worse the further you turn,
loudest at full lock — is the **power steering pump** talking
(hydraulic systems): low fluid, aerated fluid, or a tired pump. Check
the reservoir; a foamy, low, or burnt fluid tells the story, and
full-lock-held-too-long is genuinely hard on pumps. Electric-assist
cars (most new ones) skip the pump — their version is usually a
column/rack noise or motor complaint, different sound family.

## CAUSE #4 — CLUNK OVER BUMPS WHILE TURNING: LINKS, TIE RODS, BALL JOINTS

[cutaway: suspension corner — sway bar end link loose, tie rod end play, ball joint worn; clunk on articulation]

The clunk family lives where suspension meets steering: **sway bar end
links** (the most common cheap clunk), **tie rod ends**, and **ball
joints**. Turning plus a bump articulates everything at once, and worn
joints knock in their sockets. Ep 1's suspension section is cousins
with this one — vague steering and wandering often ride along. The
shop test is play-checking with the wheel jacked up; the serious end
(separating ball joint or tie rod) is a lose-steering-control failure
mode, which is why clunks earn prompt inspections even though most
turn out to be the cheap link. [TECH CONFIRM — link vs tie rod vs ball
joint frequency; bless the urgency framing]

## CAUSE #5 — CREAK AND GROAN: BUSHINGS, MOUNTS, AND THE SPRING SEAT

[cutaway: strut top mount rotating with a dry groan; control arm bushings cracked; spring seat creak]

The ship-noise tier: **strut top mounts** (the bearing your whole strut
pivots on when steering) groan and creak when dry or worn — classic
low-speed, full-lock, worse-cold pattern. Aged **control-arm bushings**
creak over the same moves. Annoying more than dangerous, usually — but
a seized strut mount also ruins steering return and tire wear, so it's
a fix-with-the-next-job item, not a never item. [TECH CONFIRM — agree
with that urgency slot? Strut-mount-with-strut-job bundling advice]

## THE REPAIR — WHAT THE MONEY LOOKS LIKE

[flow: sound in → part out → ranges]

Rough ladder, cheapest first: sway bar links and boot re-greases at
the bottom; tie rods, strut mounts, power-steering service in the
middle; CV axles and bearings mid-to-upper; ball joints varying by
design. The alignment footnote: anything that touches steering
geometry — tie rods especially — ends with an **alignment**, so that
line on the quote isn't padding. [TECH CONFIRM — bless the
always-align-after list]

## URGENT OR IGNORABLE?

[callout: three-tier verdict card — "Schedule it" / "This week" / "Inspect before highway"]

**Schedule it:** creaks and groans, mild PS whine with a healthy
reservoir top-up. Comfort-tier, on your calendar.

**This week:** clicking CV joints (they only get worse and can strand
you), recurring clunks, foamy/burnt PS fluid.

**Inspect before highway driving:** loud/growing bearing noise, any
clunk with visible steering play or wandering, or a click that's
become a shudder under power. Steering and wheel-retention parts get
the conservative call, every time.

## OUTRO

So: the turn is the interrogation, and the sound is the confession.
Clicks name the CV joint, sweeping growls name the loaded bearing,
wheel-tracking whines name the pump, bump-clunks name the linkage, and
ship-creaks name the mounts. Five sounds, five parts, and a flashlight
check of the boots that can catch the expensive one while it's cheap.
