## COLD OPEN

[broll: heat-shimmer parking lot, driver gets in, vents blast — hand held to vent, disappointment]

First real hot day of the year. You crank the AC, hold your hand to the
vent, and get... air. Moving air, sure. Cold? Not really. Maybe cool-ish
after ten minutes on the highway, maybe cold on one side and warm on
the other, maybe just a fan doing cardio.

Car AC isn't magic and it isn't a black box — it's a loop that moves
heat from inside your car to outside it, and it fails in ranked,
recognizable ways. And one PSA up front this episode will keep
repeating: the parts-store recharge can is both the cheapest fix on
this list and the most expensive mistake, depending on one question
almost nobody asks first.

[callout: channel card — "Why Is My Car Doing That?"]

## THE SYMPTOM, PRECISELY

[callout: three-way split — "Not cold at all / cool-not-cold" / "Cold sometimes (moving vs idle)" / "One side cold, one warm"]

**Version one: never really cold.** Blows fine, temperature wrong.
The classic low-refrigerant story — but "low" MEANS "leaked," hold
that thought.

**Version two: cold on the highway, warm at idle** — or cold mornings,
warm afternoons. Heat-rejection problem: condenser or its fan, usually.

**Version three: driver's side arctic, passenger side Sahara** (or
vice versa). Almost always a **blend door** — a cabin airflow flap —
not a refrigerant problem at all. Dual-zone cars are full of these.

And one more datum: does the AC button click the compressor on? Listen
for the under-hood click-and-RPM-dip when you press it. No click = the
system is refusing to engage, which is itself a diagnosis.
[TECH CONFIRM — bless the listen-for-the-click check]

## THE MECHANISM — THE LOOP IN NINETY SECONDS

[flow: NEW 3D — AC loop: compressor squeezes refrigerant hot → condenser (front of car) dumps heat outside → expansion device drops pressure → evaporator (in dash) gets COLD → cabin air blows across it → back to compressor; phase-change color coding]

Ninety seconds of honest physics: refrigerant is a fluid chosen
because it boils at useful temperatures. The **compressor** squeezes
it hot; the **condenser** (the radiator-looking thing up front) dumps
that heat to outside air; the **expansion device** lets the pressure
crash, which makes the refrigerant *very cold*; the **evaporator**
(a small radiator inside your dash) gets cold with it; your blower
pushes cabin air across it — cold vents. Loop repeats.

So cold air needs: enough refrigerant, a working squeezer, heat
actually leaving at the condenser, and cabin air actually routed
across the cold core. Four requirements. Four failure families.

## CAUSE #1 — LOW REFRIGERANT, WHICH MEANS A LEAK (the ep-6 rule returns)

[cutaway: loop with level dropping — leak points glowing: hose crimps, O-rings, condenser stone-strike, evaporator; UV dye glow]

Same law as coolant, same phrasing on purpose: **the AC loop is
sealed — refrigerant doesn't get used up, it escapes.** A system that
needs a "recharge" every spring has a leak renting space in it. Common
exits: rubber-line crimps and O-rings, a condenser holed by road
debris, or the evaporator aging out behind the dash (tell: the drain
sometimes stops dripping and the cabin smells vaguely sweet-chemical).
Shops find leaks with UV dye — same trick as the oil episode — or an
electronic sniffer. [TECH CONFIRM — your top AC leak sites by
frequency; condenser stone-strikes common?]

**The recharge-can PSA, part one:** a can on a leaky system is renting
cold by the month while venting refrigerant — which is both money and
(depending on refrigerant) not something to shrug at environmentally.
Leak-test first is the honest sequence. [TECH CONFIRM — your
counter-speech version; also R-134a vs R-1234yf cost reality — the
newer refrigerant changed recharge economics a LOT, worth numbers]

## CAUSE #2 — COMPRESSOR AND CLUTCH (the no-click and the racket)

[cutaway: compressor — clutch face engaging/not; seized internals; metal debris path through loop]

The squeezer fails two ways. Quietly: the **clutch** never engages (no
click) — sometimes just a low-refrigerant lockout protecting itself,
sometimes electrical, sometimes the clutch itself. Loudly: bearing
racket, rattles, or a compressor that grenades internally and — the
expensive sentence — sends metal debris through the whole loop, which
is why a proper compressor replacement includes flushing and often a
new condenser/dryer. The "cheap compressor swap" that skips the flush
is a repeat customer generator. [TECH CONFIRM — bless the
full-service-not-just-the-part framing; how often do you see debris-
contaminated systems from prior cheap swaps?]

## CAUSE #3 — HEAT REJECTION: CONDENSER AIRFLOW AND FANS (the at-idle pattern)

[flow: condenser airflow — highway ram air vs idle fan dependence; fins packed with bugs/plastic bag; fan dead]

Version-two symptoms live here: cold while moving, warm at idle means
the condenser only sheds heat with ram air — its **fan** isn't pulling
(same fan story as the overheating episode; the systems share
hardware on many cars) or its fins are packed with bugs, cottonwood
fluff, or the classic plastic shopping bag. A garden-hose fin rinse
(gentle, straight-on) is legitimate owner maintenance. [TECH CONFIRM —
bless the fin-rinse advice with your cautions]

## CAUSE #4 — CABIN AIRFLOW: BLEND DOORS, CABIN FILTER, AND THE MOLDY CORE

[cutaway: dash airflow — blend door actuator clicking/stuck half-open; cabin filter black with debris; evaporator core furred]

Refrigerant loop perfect, cabin plumbing wrong: **blend door
actuators** (little motors aiming flaps) fail with a signature
*click-click-click behind the dash* and cause the one-side-warm split
or heat stuck on. A **choked cabin filter** — the filter everyone
forgets exists — strangles total airflow so even cold air arrives
weak. And a furred, moldy **evaporator** smells gym-sock and blocks
flow. Cabin filter is glovebox-DIY on most cars; check yours today.

## URGENT OR IGNORABLE?

[callout: three-tier verdict card — "Comfort tier" / "Book it" / "Shut it off"]

**Comfort tier:** most of this episode. An AC that's merely warm
strands nobody — fix it when the calendar and wallet agree. (Exception
carve-out: heat waves are genuinely dangerous for kids, elders, and
long commutes — comfort-tier doesn't mean trivial.)

**Book it:** the every-spring-recharge pattern (that's a leak on a
payment plan), at-idle warmth (fan issues also threaten engine
cooling — ep 5 cousin), or any compressor noise.

**Shut it off:** grinding/rattling from the compressor area, or AC
engagement that stalls or screeches the engine. Running a dying
compressor is how a part becomes a whole-loop contamination job.

## OUTRO

So: cold air is a loop doing four jobs, and the failure pattern names
the job — never-cold says leak, idle-warm says airflow outside,
one-side-warm says a flap inside, and no-click says the system is
protecting itself or refusing. The recharge can without a leak test is
renting cold; the gauge-and-dye visit is buying it back. And check
that cabin filter — today, seriously, it's three minutes.
