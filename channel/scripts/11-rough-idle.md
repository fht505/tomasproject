## COLD OPEN

[broll: interior at a red light — coffee cup in the holder rippling, wheel trembling faintly]

Red light. Foot on the brake. And the car... shivers. The wheel hums in
your hands, the coffee ripples like that scene in Jurassic Park, and
the moment you pull away — smooth again, like nothing happened.

Idle is the engine's hardest trick: keeping sixty-plus explosions a
second perfectly balanced with NO throttle to hide behind. Everything
that's slightly wrong shows up here first, at the light, in your coffee
cup. Which makes rough idle the great early-warning symptom — annoying
long before it's expensive, if you listen while it's cheap.

[callout: channel card — "Why Is My Car Doing That?"]

## THE SYMPTOM, PRECISELY

[callout: three-way split — "Shake in the car, or in the RPM?" / "Cold only, warm only, always?" / "AC on/off change?"]

**Watch the tach at idle.** If the NEEDLE is steady but the CAR shakes,
that's a vibration transmission problem — spoiler, motor mounts — not a
combustion problem. If the needle itself wanders, dips, or surges, the
engine is genuinely struggling.

**Temperature pattern.** Rough ONLY cold then smooths warm; rough ONLY
warm; rough always. Cold-rough leans rich-mixture and sticky-valve
suspects; always-rough leans vacuum and ignition.

**Load test at a standstill:** flip the AC on. AC adds load; a marginal
idle gets visibly worse. Headlights, defroster — same trick. Note it;
it's free evidence. [TECH CONFIRM — bless AC-flip as a useful at-home
data point]

## THE MECHANISM — WHY IDLE EXPOSES EVERYTHING

[flow: metronome from ep 10 slowed way down — at idle, each combustion event stands alone; one weak beat rocks the whole engine]

At 700 RPM there's no momentum to blur mistakes. Every cylinder's
contribution is felt individually; one weak or missing beat rocks the
engine on its mounts — the exact lope you're feeling. And because idle
airflow is tiny, even a small unmetered leak is a HUGE percentage
error at idle while being a rounding error at speed. That asymmetry is
why so many problems idle rough but drive fine.

## CAUSE #1 — VACUUM LEAKS (the idle specialist)

[cutaway: intake tract from ep 10 — cracked hose, brittle intake boot, failed intake gasket; hiss visualized; idle air % math overlay]

The signature: **rough idle that smooths out the second you rev.**
At 700 RPM, a pencil-size leak might be 10% of total airflow — chaos.
At 3,000 RPM it's 1% — invisible. The engine bay is full of aging
rubber: vacuum hoses, intake boots, gaskets, brake booster line. Shops
find leaks with smoke machines — literally filling the intake with
theatrical smoke and watching where it escapes. [TECH CONFIRM — smoke
test standard at FHT? Price for the callout table]

## CAUSE #2 — IGNITION MISFIRE AT IDLE (plugs, coils — the ep-10 suspects, second shift)

[cutaway: coil/plug stack — weak spark at low RPM, cylinder-specific lope; misfire counter]

Same ignition suspects as the acceleration episode, different shift:
a marginal plug or coil that survives cruise can drop beats at idle
where combustion is least forgiving. Tell: rhythmic lope (you can
almost count the bad cylinder), often a P030X code, sometimes a
flashing light at idle. The coil-swap test from ep 10 applies
unchanged. [TECH CONFIRM — anything idle-specific you'd add vs ep 10's

## CAUSE #3 — DIRTY THROTTLE BODY AND IDLE CONTROL

[cutaway: throttle body — carbon ring around the plate; airflow strangled at the tiny idle opening]

At idle the throttle plate is nearly closed; the computer meters air
through a sliver of an opening. Years of PCV vapors (hello, ep 8) bake
a carbon ring exactly where that sliver lives, strangling and
destabilizing idle airflow: surging idle, stalls at stops, rough after
battery disconnects. The fix is famously satisfying — a cleaning — with
one modern caution: electronic throttle bodies want a **relearn
procedure** after, or the idle gets worse before it gets better.

cars? Your relearn horror stories welcome]

## CAUSE #4 — MOTOR MOUNTS (the steady-tach impostor)

[cutaway: NEW insert — engine on its mounts; one collapsed hydraulic mount; vibration path into the frame at idle-in-Drive]

The impostor from the symptom section: tach steady, combustion fine,
car still shakes — worst at idle IN GEAR, foot on brake, often
smoothing in Park or Neutral. That's a collapsed **motor mount** — the
rubber-and-fluid cushions that carry the engine. Free driveway test:
idle in Drive, brake held vs Neutral — big difference points at
mounts. [TECH CONFIRM — bless the in-gear-vs-neutral test; typical
mount job cost range]

## CAUSE #5 — FUEL TRIM DRIFT: INJECTORS, SENSORS, AND THE CHECK-ENGINE BREADCRUMBS

[flow: fuel trim graph drifting positive; O2 sensor lazy; one injector dribbling]

The subtle tier: aging **O2 sensors** report lazily, a tired
**injector** dribbles instead of misting, the computer chases its own
tail, and idle quality erodes gradually — usually with fuel-trim or
lean codes as breadcrumbs. This tier is scan-tool territory; the
takeaway for viewers is just that gradual-and-getting-worse with
brea dcrumb codes is a real, findable pattern, not "cars just do
that." [TECH CONFIRM — comfortable keeping this tier brief like this?]

## THE REPAIR — WHAT THE MONEY LOOKS LIKE

[flow: decision ladder — steady-tach? mounts. wanders? scan → smoke test → cheap items → sensors]

The fork saves the money: **steady tach + shaking car → mounts** —
skip the engine diagnosis entirely. **Wandering tach → scan, smoke
test, ignition basics, throttle cleaning** — in that order, cheapest
evidence first. Most rough idles die on the first two rungs.

## URGENT OR IGNORABLE?

[callout: three-tier verdict card — "Annoyance tier" / "Book it" / "Same rules as ep 10"]

**Annoyance tier:** mild, consistent, no codes, no stalls — a mount
story or minor leak. Fix on your schedule; it won't strand you.

**Book it:** stalling at stops, surging, any code, or the pattern
getting worse month over month. Stalls in traffic are a safety issue
dressed as an annoyance.

**Same rules as ep 10:** flashing light, raw fuel smell — the misfire
urgency tiers apply verbatim.

## OUTRO

So: idle is the engine with nowhere to hide. The tach sorts real
combustion trouble from mount impostors, the rev test fingers vacuum
leaks, the AC-flip adds free evidence, and the smoke machine finds
what ears can't. Catch it at the coffee-ripple stage and it's almost
always bottom-of-the-ladder money.
