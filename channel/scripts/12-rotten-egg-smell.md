## COLD OPEN

[broll: driver's POV, nose wrinkle beat — window cracked at a stoplight]

It hits you at a red light, or halfway up an on-ramp: sulfur. Rotten
eggs. That middle-school-chemistry-class smell, riding along with you.

Here's what's interesting about this one: your nose is doing real
chemistry. Gasoline contains sulfur; a healthy exhaust system processes
it into something odorless; and when you smell egg, a specific device
under your car is telling you it's overwhelmed — usually because of a
problem UPSTREAM of it. The smell is rarely the sick part. It's the
messenger.

[callout: channel card — "Why Is My Car Doing That?"]

## THE SYMPTOM, PRECISELY

[callout: three-way split — "Outside/exhaust smell?" / "Under-hood, near the battery?" / "Only under hard acceleration?"]

Three sniff-tests before anything else:

**Where's it strongest?** Behind the car near the tailpipe: exhaust
story — the main one tonight. Under the hood, sharp and acrid, near
one corner: that's not exhaust at all — that's a **battery** story, and
it's the short, urgent detour we'll take first.

**When?** Only under hard acceleration or full load: classic
running-rich-plus-converter pattern. All the time, even at idle: the
converter's likely further gone.

**Any companions?** Check engine light, worse fuel economy, sluggish
acceleration — write down which ones ride along; they matter.

## THE DETOUR FIRST — A LEAKING OR OVERCHARGED BATTERY

[cutaway: battery insert — vent caps, sulfuric-acid electrolyte boiling under overcharge, terminal corrosion]

Quick but important: battery electrolyte is sulfuric acid, and an
**overcharging** (failing voltage regulator) or dying, hot battery can
boil and vent it — a sharp sulfur smell UNDER THE HOOD, sometimes with
a hot-plastic edge, crusty terminal corrosion, or a swollen case. This
one isn't a converter conversation; it's a charging-system-today
conversation — venting acid near fuel lines and wiring earns urgency.
Under-hood egg smell: battery and charging check first, full episode on
the battery light later this season. [TECH CONFIRM — how often does
"egg smell" turn out battery vs converter at FHT?]

## THE MECHANISM — WHAT THE CONVERTER ACTUALLY DOES

[cutaway: NEW 3D — catalytic converter: honeycomb core, precious-metal wash coat, exhaust flowing through; sulfur chemistry overlay H2S → SO2]

The **catalytic converter** is a ceramic honeycomb coated in precious
metals, sitting in your exhaust stream, finishing combustion's leftover
chemistry. Sulfur's path: a healthy converter oxidizes smelly hydrogen
sulfide into odorless sulfur dioxide. When the converter is
**overwhelmed or dying**, that conversion stops — and you smell the
eggs. Two ways it gets there, and the order matters:

## CAUSE #1 — RUNNING RICH: THE UPSTREAM PROBLEM (fix this first)

[flow: rich mixture pouring excess fuel into the converter — overheating core, glowing; upstream culprits highlighted: O2 sensor, injector, coolant temp sensor, misfires]

An engine running **rich** — too much fuel — sends unburnt fuel into
the converter, which tries to burn it, overheats, and falls behind on
its chemistry: egg smell under load, often with worse mileage and a
check engine light (rich or O2 codes). Usual instigators: aging **O2
sensors**, a leaking **injector**, a bad **coolant temp sensor** lying
about warm-up, or the misfires from episode 10 washing cylinders
through.

Here's the money-saving order of operations: a converter replaced
WITHOUT fixing the rich condition dies again — expensively, on
schedule. Upstream first, always. [TECH CONFIRM — your version of the
"they replaced the cat twice" story; it's a legendary pattern]

## CAUSE #2 — THE CONVERTER ITSELF: POISONED, MELTED, OR AGED OUT

[cutaway: converter cores side by side — healthy honeycomb / melted-clogged channels / rattling broken substrate]

Sometimes the messenger IS sick: cores get **melted** by chronic rich
running or misfires, **poisoned** by coolant (head gasket episodes,
hello) or oil (burning-oil episode, hello), or simply age out of
efficiency — the P0420/P0430 "catalyst efficiency" codes. Companion
tells of a genuinely dying converter: sulfur smell PLUS sluggishness
(a clogging core chokes the engine's breathing), rattle from beneath
(broken substrate), or glowing-red converter after driving —
[TECH CONFIRM — the restricted-exhaust symptoms you check first: vacuum
gauge? backpressure test?]

And the elephant: converters are the theft-magnet part (the precious
metals), and the pricey part. Which makes the fix-upstream-first rule
above the single most valuable sentence in this episode.

## CAUSE #3 — FUEL QUALITY AND THE FLUID IMPOSTORS

[callout: short-list card — high-sulfur fill-up · gear-oil leak on hot exhaust · trans fluid overheat]

The minor suspects, quickly: a tank of **high-sulfur fuel** can smell
eggy for a tank and vanish — same run-it-low-and-refill advice as ep
10. And two impostor smells: **gear oil** (hypoid oil is sulfur-based;
a differential or manual-box leak dripping on hot exhaust smells
rotten-adjacent) and severely **overheated transmission fluid**. Both
come with their own evidence — stains, ep-7 rules. [TECH CONFIRM —
gear-oil-on-exhaust as an impostor you actually see?]

## THE REPAIR — WHAT THE MONEY LOOKS LIKE

[flow: order of operations — scan → fix rich condition → THEN evaluate converter → aftermarket vs OEM decision]

The ladder: **scan and fix upstream** (sensor/injector money) →
**re-evaluate the converter after** the engine runs clean — some
recover usable function, many efficiency codes persist → **converter
replacement** as the informed last step, with the aftermarket-vs-OEM
quality conversation had honestly (cheap cats often fail efficiency
codes within a year or two [TECH CONFIRM — your aftermarket cat

## URGENT OR IGNORABLE?

[callout: three-tier verdict card — "One-tank watch" / "This week" / "Today"]

**One-tank watch:** first-time smell right after a fill-up, no light,
no other symptoms. Run it down, refill elsewhere, reassess.

**This week:** recurring smell, check engine light, mileage dropping.
The longer a rich condition runs, the more converter you burn.

**Today:** under-hood acid smell (battery/charging), rattle-plus-smell,
power loss climbing hills, or anything glowing red underneath after a
drive. Restricted exhaust and venting acid both earn same-day
attention.

## OUTRO

So: rotten eggs is chemistry your converter stopped finishing — usually
because something upstream is feeding it too much fuel. Fix the sender,
then judge the messenger; never buy the converter first. And if the
smell lives under the hood instead of behind the car, it's battery
acid, and today's the day.
